01 · Company

Connectivity is infrastructure.

Reliable internet access has become essential infrastructure for education, commerce, communication and modern work. Yet many communities — student housing in particular — remain poorly served by conventional connectivity infrastructure, despite dense, consistent demand.

Z-Network's first network serves off-campus lodge compounds near a university in Lafia, Nasarawa State. Rather than laying fixed-line infrastructure, we anchor each hub to a Starlink connection, then distribute it locally over MikroTik-managed wireless access points — bringing a functioning ISP to a compound in days, not months.

"The first network is our proof of execution. The larger opportunity is a repeatable model for connecting communities — one compound, one hub at a time."
02 · The problem

Large connectivity gaps remain.

Infrastructure

Traditional broadband infrastructure requires significant capital, civil works and long deployment timelines that don't fit fast-turnover student housing.

Coverage

Compounds outside the immediate footprint of major fixed-line networks stay underserved, even where hundreds of paying tenants live within a few hundred metres of each other.

Economics

Low-density or informally-managed locations rarely attract conventional ISP investment quickly enough to satisfy local demand.

The opportunity is not simply selling bandwidth. It is building the infrastructure that delivers it.
03 · Our solution

A practical model for local connectivity.

Z-Network designs localized connectivity networks around the economics and physical realities of each community, then runs the whole customer lifecycle through software we built ourselves.

Upstream connectivity

Starlink provides backhaul where fibre is unavailable or impractical, with a defined path to Starlink Business Priority or a dedicated line as compound count grows.

Wireless distribution

MikroTik-managed access points distribute connectivity across each compound, with hotspot profiles controlling speed per plan tier.

Localized operations

Local deployment and support reduce the complexity of operating a distributed network across multiple sites.

Self-service by WhatsApp

Customers renew, get receipts and receive expiry reminders through a WhatsApp bot running on-site — no app to install.

Payments & automation

An admin dashboard and customer portal connect Paystack payments, voucher stock, renewals and expiry tracking — no manual bookkeeping.

Measured expansion

Every deployment feeds real usage and revenue data back into the model, informing the economics of the next hub before we build it.

06 · Technology

Technology built for operational control.

Hardware layer

Starlink kits, MikroTik routers, PoE-switched wireless access points and the cabling and power infrastructure each hub needs to run unattended.

Network layer

RouterOS hotspot authentication, per-plan bandwidth profiles, and a polling service that pushes commands — renewals, suspensions, profile changes — to every router roughly once a minute.

Software layer

An admin dashboard and customer portal on Supabase Postgres, a WhatsApp bot for receipts and renewal reminders, PDF sales reporting, and live access-point monitoring — all built in-house.

Our objective is not to build technology for its own sake. Technology exists to make distributed connectivity infrastructure easier to operate — and cheaper to scale.

11 · Questions investors ask

Straight answers before you commit capital.

How does the 45% revenue share actually work?

Every access point you fund is tracked individually in our database. When a customer buys a voucher on that AP, the sale is attributed to it directly — not pooled with the rest of the network — so your 45% is calculated off real, per-AP revenue, not an estimate.

Is this equity, or a loan?

Neither. It's a direct revenue-share tied to a specific piece of infrastructure you fund. You don't own shares in Z-Network, and we don't owe you the principal back on a schedule — your return comes from that AP's ongoing revenue, for as long as it operates.

What happens if an access point underperforms, or the network shuts down?

Revenue share is only paid on revenue actually collected — there's no guaranteed minimum. We're formalizing buyback provisions as part of our investor agreements, so investors have a defined exit path rather than an open-ended commitment. Ask us directly for the current terms before committing capital.

Is Z-Network a registered company?

Yes — Z-Network is CAC registered. We're also completing SMEDAN registration alongside it, so revenue-share agreements sit on a proper legal structure.

What's the biggest operational risk right now?

Uplink capacity. Starlink Residential has a practical ceiling once you're running several compounds off one hub, especially during rain fade. We're evaluating Starlink Business Priority and a Galaxy Backbone dedicated line specifically to remove this constraint before further expansion.

How is my access point protected from being kicked offline or reused?

Every customer session ties back to a permanent voucher code in our system, and access-point uptime is logged automatically, with history you can review. Revenue and infrastructure records for investor-funded APs are kept separate from general operating stock.
